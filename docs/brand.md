# Brand guide

## Position

BenchLedger helps makers know what they have, plan what to build, and return the
result to an accurate inventory. It is an evidence-backed workbench, not a
warehouse dashboard or an autonomous purchasing agent.

BenchLedger was selected after a preliminary exact-name search across the web,
GitHub, and npm found no collision. That check is practical release diligence,
not a formal trademark opinion.

## Personality

**Calm, precise, ingenious.** The product should feel trustworthy enough for
engineering work, welcoming enough for a first project, and quietly satisfying
during repeated use.

## Message

- Tagline: **Know what you have. Plan what to build. Close the loop.**
- Short description: **Inventory and project planning for 3D printing,
  electronics, and AI-assisted making.**
- Lead with the build decision, not the database.
- Say what is known, what is inferred, and what needs a physical check.

## Mark

The three ascending ledger bars represent **stock → plan → build**. Their open
workbench frame keeps the symbol grounded in making rather than finance. Use the
standalone mark at small sizes and the horizontal lockup in documentation.

Assets:

- [`benchledger-mark.svg`](assets/benchledger-mark.svg)
- [`benchledger-lockup.svg`](assets/benchledger-lockup.svg)

Keep clear space equal to one bar width around the mark. Do not rotate it, apply
gradients, or add shadows.

## Color

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workbench ink | `#173A35` | Primary dark background and text |
| Ledger moss | `#3F795C` | Actions and positive evidence |
| Signal mint | `#9CD3A9` | Mark detail and selected state |
| Paper | `#F7F9F6` | Product and documentation surface |
| Safety amber | `#C58A25` | Inspect-first and uncertainty only |

Color never carries inventory or risk meaning by itself. Pair it with text and
an icon, and preserve WCAG 2.2 AA contrast for functional content.

## Voice

Prefer short, plain labels: **Ready to use**, **Check quantity**, **Partly
covered**, and **Need to buy**. Avoid database jargon, fake certainty, hype,
anthropomorphic AI claims, and language that implies a purchase or physical
verification happened when it did not.
